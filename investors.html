Competitive Intelligence

Landscape

Analysis based on pitch decks, investor memos, and call notes · Confidential · March 2026

Market Context
The AI-native services roll-up category is forming rapidly. Three distinct archetypes have emerged: (1) single-vertical tech-enabled roll-ups with future SaaS aspirations (Splash), (2) broad SMB holdcos built around operator networks (American Operator), and (3) AI-first institutional platforms with frontier model partnerships (Thrive Holdings). Orbit is the only player combining a payments data flywheel, a live proprietary AI stack, permanent capital, and a recurring field services thesis.

What They're Doing
Founded April 2025 by Thrive Capital to acquire and operate companies in accounting and IT services — the same MSP vertical as Orbit's Apex MSP Group.
OpenAI took an equity stake in Dec 2025 and embeds cross-functional teams (research, product, engineering) directly inside portfolio companies to build AI from within.
Deployed $100M into Shield (IT services platform) via Feb 2026 raise; Crete (accounting) received $500M for AI-driven roll-up.
Stated goal: establish a repeatable, scalable AI deployment model across operationally intensive service industries — then expand to additional sectors.
Strengths
Frontier AI access — OpenAI embedded inside portfolio companies, not just API access.
Institutional capital at scale — $600M+ deployed, vs. Orbit's early stage.
IT services focus overlaps directly with Orbit's MSP vertical.
Weaknesses
No payments data moat — Thrive does not own the payment layer across portfolio companies.
VC-backed structure means forced exits; no permanent compounding capital advantage.
AI is externally deployed (OpenAI teams visit); not a proprietary stack trained on proprietary data.
Accounting + IT focus limits cross-vertical data flywheel; verticals share little operational DNA.
Orbit's edge: Payments ownership across all portfolio companies creates a cross-portfolio data moat Thrive cannot replicate. OpenAI access is a commodity — payment transaction data is not. Permanent capital means Orbit can out-hold any VC-backed competitor.

Public holding company designed to match industry-veteran operators with Main Street SMB acquisition targets across diversified industries (plumbing, HVAC, landscaping, home services, manufacturing).
Operator-first model: instead of ETA/MBA buyers, they source vetted industry veterans from a 5,100+ operator network to run acquired businesses. Operator carries personal guarantees; American Operator holds majority board control.
Private credit arm mirrors SBA underwriting; finances acquisitions with seller notes + term debt. No outside PE capital required per deal.
Technology platform handles financial reporting (GL-level API), cap table management, and distribution waterfall calculations across portfolio.
Brand assets: "Locally Owned & Operated" certification, SMB15 podcast, media brand — used for deal sourcing and operator recruitment.
Operator network grew from ~870 to 5,125 in six months (Apr–Sep 2025), suggesting rapid momentum heading into 2026.
Strengths
Scale and track record — 99 transactions, $300M+ TEV, 2,400 businesses reviewed. Significant head start.
Operator network as proprietary deal flow and talent moat — hard to replicate quickly.
Diversified industry exposure reduces single-vertical risk.
Media brand and podcast (SMB15) creates organic deal sourcing and operator recruitment at low cost.
Weaknesses
No AI stack — technology is basic financial reporting and cap table tooling. No proprietary AI operating layer.
No payments moat — diversified industries mean no cross-portfolio data flywheel is possible.
Operator-dependent model creates key-person risk at every portfolio company; governance complexity scales poorly.
Diversification is a double-edged sword — no deep vertical expertise or compound data advantage within any one sector.
Public structure creates liquidity and reporting overhead; less nimble than a private holdco.
Orbit's edge: American Operator is building breadth; Orbit is building depth. Nexus AI and payments data compound strongest within verticals — Orbit's focused field services approach creates moats American Operator's industry-agnostic model structurally cannot. They compete on operator talent; Orbit competes on data and automation.
