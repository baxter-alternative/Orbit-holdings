Acquisition Criteria

What we're
looking for.

⟳
Recurring B2B Services
Contract-based or subscription revenue with predictable renewal cycles. No transactional, project-based, or one-time revenue businesses. The recurring model is the prerequisite — it's what makes payments data meaningful and AR automation valuable.
Monthly or annual contracts · High renewal rates · Invoice-driven
◈
AI Defensibility
The business must operate in a vertical where proprietary data creates durable advantage — not just where AI can cut costs. We look for companies sitting on untapped transaction, behavioral, or operational data that Nexus can train on and compound over time.
Proprietary data assets · Vertical-specific workflows · Model trainability
◉
Relationship-Led, Automation-Ready
The client relationship is human and in-person — that's the moat. But the back office, billing, collections, scheduling, support, and reporting are ripe for full automation. We're not replacing the relationship; we're eliminating everything around it that shouldn't require a human.
Field services · Account management · High-touch client retention
⌖
Significant Tech Debt
Legacy systems, manual processes, spreadsheet-based ops, disconnected tools. The more underinvested the technology, the greater the margin expansion opportunity when Nexus deploys. We aren't intimidated by complexity — we've productized the integration playbook.
Manual AR · Disconnected CRM · No BI layer · Legacy billing
↗
Mid Single-Digit Organic Growth
We're not chasing hypergrowth. Steady, durable 4–7% organic revenue growth in a stable vertical signals a healthy business without the valuation premium of a high-growth SaaS company. Growth accelerates post-acquisition as Nexus Sales and CX tools deploy.
4–7% organic growth · Stable customer base · Low churn
◐
EBITDA Margins: 15–20% → 30%+
We target businesses with healthy but not optimized margins. 15–20% at entry signals a real business with pricing power. The path to 30%+ is repeatable: AP payment processing economics, Nexus automation replacing manual headcount, shared services at the holdco level, and low-cost geography arbitrage. Margin expansion is the return engine.
Entry: 15–20% EBITDA · Target: 30%+ · Timeline: 24–36 months
⋮⋮
Fragmented Market with M&A Runway
The ideal vertical has hundreds of sub-scale operators with no dominant platform player. Fragmentation means acquisition multiples stay low, integration playbooks are reusable, and Orbit can consolidate market share systematically. Each acquisition expands the data flywheel and improves underwriting for the next one.
No dominant player · Sub-scale operators · Replicable deal thesis

Why two raises instead of one
↑
NAV compounds at the GP level. Each successful sub-level raise at a higher valuation marks up Orbit's NAV — giving holdco investors a return signal without a forced exit.
⊕
Two bites of the apple. Orbit earns returns both as a GP (holdco appreciation) and as a majority operator (subsidiary cash flows and exits). Sub-level investors fund growth without diluting the holdco.
⚙
Control is non-negotiable. 70–80% ownership means Orbit can mandate Nexus, mandate AP as processor, and mandate data sharing upward — without founder consent. The flywheel runs on control, not cooperation.
◎
Clean related-party structure. AP's economics flow through a commercial agreement at the subsidiary level — arms-length, auditable, and conflict-free. No cross-ownership that creates investor confusion or governance disputes.
